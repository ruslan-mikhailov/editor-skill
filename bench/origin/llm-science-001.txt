The sky looks blue because of Rayleigh scattering. Sunlight contains all visible wavelengths, but as it passes through the atmosphere, air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths (blue and violet) much more strongly than longer ones (red and orange), roughly in proportion to the inverse fourth power of wavelength. Violet light is scattered even more than blue, but the sun emits less violet light, and our eyes are more sensitive to blue, so the scattered light we perceive from every direction overhead appears blue. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through much more atmosphere before reaching us, so most of the blue is scattered out of the direct beam and the remaining light looks red and orange.
