Redis and Memcached are both in-memory key-value stores often used for caching, but they differ in scope. Memcached is deliberately minimal: it stores strings (or blobs) under keys, is multithreaded, has no persistence, and scales horizontally by having clients hash keys across nodes. That simplicity makes it fast and predictable for plain object caching at high concurrency. Redis offers much more: rich data structures like hashes, lists, sets, sorted sets, and streams, plus persistence through snapshots or an append-only log, replication, pub/sub, transactions, Lua scripting, and built-in clustering. Its command execution is largely single-threaded, though recent versions add I/O threading. In practice, Memcached is a good fit when you want a simple, memory-efficient cache for serialized objects, while Redis is the better choice when you need structured data, atomic operations on it, durability, or messaging alongside caching.
